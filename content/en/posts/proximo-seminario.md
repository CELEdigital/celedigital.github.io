The seminar will address the implications of the shift toward infrastructure in Internet governance: a policy change in which states pursue sovereignty through tangible interventions in the physical and technical architecture of the Internet. In the [article to be discussed](https://www.palermo.edu/Archivos_content/2026/derecho/octubre/seminario-cele-soberania-digital/pdf-cele-documento.pdf), a typology of six strategies is proposed that nation-states implement at central points within the Internet’s infrastructure, referred to as “control points.”

These strategies include subsidizing new network connections to bypass certain nodes, adding a neutralizing layer around control points, fragmenting key nodes, diversifying governance, taking control of the control point, or creating smaller local nodes. Each strategy is illustrated with an example of how a nation-state implemented it in a specific context.

The typology proposes an analytical framework for examining Internet governance preferences, while offering policymakers in each country a practical roadmap for promoting strategic digital autonomy, resisting coercion, and shaping initiatives such as the Non-Aligned Movement’s Technology Initiative. By focusing on how governments exercise power over infrastructure, the article contributes to debates on digital sovereignty and decolonization. It presents a narrative that goes beyond the political paralysis generated by fears of a fragmentation of the global information network we call the Internet.
